Research Credentialing Process Redesign

Completed
Problem
The institutional process for credentialing non-employee study team personnel and student researchers was manual, inconsistent, and difficult to scale, creating delays in research startup and gaps in compliance visibility.
Approach
Facilitated Voice of Customer (VOC) sessions with stakeholders and developed a current-state process map to identify root causes of rework and delay.
Actions
Redesigned and automated the credentialing workflow to eliminate non-value-added steps; built ongoing performance metrics to monitor throughput and compliance; developed a Control Plan — new education and updated policy/SOPs — to sustain gains and prevent regression; rolled out training and communications across the entire research community.
Impact
Significantly reduced rework and lead time in the credentialing process, establishing a scalable, standardized workflow now used institution-wide, supporting, 800+ applicants annually.

FDA Submission Oversight Initiative

In Progress
Problem
The institution lacked structured oversight of Investigator-Initiated FDA-regulated studies, creating the risk of poor application quality, non-compliance, and inconsistant understanding of regulatory requirements among investigators.
Approach
Conducted a gap analysis with institutional leadership, legal, and regulatory specialists to identify root causes of inconsistent submission quality and oversight gaps in FDA-regulated, sponsor-investigator studies.
Actions
Built a new submission tracking workflow to establish standard work across the submission process; developed supporting policies to close identified gaps; created a Control Plan consisting of two education sessions for Sponsor-Investigators and two comprehensive guidance documents, sustaining improved application quality and regulatory understanding going forward.
Impact to date
Improved consistency and quality of FDA submissions; strengthened Sponsor-Investigators' working knowledge of federally mandated responsibilities.

Green Belt Capstone: Compliance–Education Alignment

In Progress · Expected March 2027
Problem
Research education programming has not been systematically informed by compliance audit data, resulting in a disconnect between where noncompliance risk is highest and where education resources are targeted.
Approach
Applying DMAIC methodology to analyze audit findings data by department and noncompliance trends to identify the highest-priority gaps.
Actions
Developing a targeted marketing and education delivery strategy aligned to departments and most frequent compliance findings; to be piloted as part of Green Belt certification.
Goal
Reduce recurring compliance findings by aligning education investment with data-driven priority areas.
